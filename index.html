Coming Home LA

A plain, step-by-step guide for your first 90 days after release. Check things off as you go. Your checkmarks stay on this device only.

1

First 72 hours

Safety, food, and staying in good standing.

Go when your release papers say. Bring your release paperwork. Ask your officer about programs, a DMV fee waiver, and bus passes while you're there.

211 LA connects you to shelters and interim housing across the county, any time of day.

211la.orgCall 2-1-1

Food Oasis LA maps free pantries and meals near you. CalFresh can sometimes be approved in as little as 3 days in an emergency, so apply right away (see Week 1).

foodoasis.la

You'll need a number for jobs, benefits, and your officer. California LifeLine offers discounted phone service to people with low income or on programs like CalFresh or Medi-Cal.

californialifeline.com
2

Week 1

Papers and benefits. These unlock almost everything else.

If you left state prison through the CAL-ID program, you may already have one. If not, make a DMV appointment. Your probation officer can help you apply for a fee waiver.

dmv.ca.govAbout CAL-ID

Most jobs ask for it. Request a replacement from Social Security.

ssa.gov

One application on BenefitsCal covers food (CalFresh / EBT), health care (Medi-Cal), and cash aid (General Relief or CalWORKs). You can also apply by phone with LA County DPSS.

benefitscal.comDPSS: 866-613-3777
3

First month

Get a team behind you: case management, work, and recovery support.

The county's Justice, Care and Opportunities Department (JCOD) runs free programs: case managers with lived experience, housing help, legal aid, and paid job training. There's also a program just for women, and one for young adults 18 to 26.

jcod.lacounty.govJusticeConnect.org833-522-5263

Antelope Valley: ask about the D.O.O.R.S. reentry center, which also links people to free record-clearing help.

JCOD's SECTOR program offers training and paid work experience. Homeboy Industries in downtown LA hires and trains people coming home and offers case management.

homeboyindustries.org

The county's Substance Abuse Service Helpline screens you and refers you to treatment, including outpatient, residential, and medication programs. Medi-Cal covers most of it.

LA County SAPC1-844-804-7500
4

Months 2 to 3

Clear the path ahead.

The LA County Public Defender offers free expungement help. California expanded which convictions qualify in 2023, so ask even if you were told no before. Some old felonies may also be reduced under Prop 47.

pubdef.lacounty.gov213-204-9904

In California, your right to vote comes back once you finish your prison term, even if you're on parole or probation.

registertovote.ca.gov

Set goals for stable housing, steady income, and school or training. Bring this checklist so they can see what's already done.
